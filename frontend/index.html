01 / Approach

The terms are only
half the story.

A financing contract connects payments to a business that changes over time. RBF examines that connection without treating a model as evidence of what real merchants will do.

01

Start with the contract.

Identify the advance, remittance basis, payment schedule and repayment target. A cap factor describes a target—not an annual interest rate.

02

Change the revenue path.

Inspect stable trading, declining sales and closure scenarios. Observe how the specified payment rules respond under the model’s assumptions.

03

Read both sides.

Consider seller payment burden alongside provider recovery. Payment as a share of revenue does not establish affordability; incomplete target recovery is not a default prediction.

02 / Simulation Lab

Follow the result
back to its assumptions.

Compare fixed-payment and revenue-based contracts using registered simulation outputs. Each scenario brings the burden, recovery and settlement views together.

Scope & limitations

A few important
distinctions.

Is RBF a lender?+

No. RBF holds no capital, makes no credit offers and is not a lending service. It is independent research and a software demonstration.

Does the study predict who will repay?+

No. It examines contractual mechanics under specified revenue scenarios. It does not establish creditworthiness, predict default or assess suitability for a particular merchant.

Do these scenarios describe Vietnamese merchants?+

No. Vietnamese e-commerce motivates the research, but the scenario parameters are modelling assumptions, not estimates from a representative seller dataset.

Can I upload merchant records?+

Not here. There is no historical-record upload on this website. Use synthetic examples only in the prototype; its submission flow can store records and send administrator notifications.
